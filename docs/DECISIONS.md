# Decisions

Choices made where `docs/GAME_SPEC.md` is silent or allows more than one option. Each entry says what was chosen, why, and what it would take to change. Newest at the bottom.

| D-010 | Server hit detection samples the weapon's path at 60 Hz during each damage window and runs `workspace:Spherecast` between successive samples (a swept capsule approximated by spheres). The 150 ms lag allowance is a time rewind: a requested Art or basic swing starts its server timeline at the request's `clientTime` (stamped with `workspace:GetServerTimeNow()`, the clock the server reads), clamped by `CombatService.runStartOf(clientTime, receipt, freeAt)` into `[max(receipt - lagAllowance, min(freeAt, receipt)), receipt]`, where `freeAt` is the fighter's `lockUntil` read before the new action replaces it. The facing check is a separate cosine slack (`combat.facingTolerance`). | Roblox has no capsule sweep primitive; spherecasts along the path are cheap and deterministic on the server. Section 6 asks for a 150 ms lag allowance on the sweep; section 17 says an Art cannot be requested more often than its clip allows, so the rewind is floored at the previous lock's end (unbounded, an early stamp fit about 19% more Arts). | Replace `HitSweep.luau` with a shapecast when Roblox ships one; tune `lagAllowance` in `combat.luau`. |

| D-044 | The server sweep follows the blade's arc. Between consecutive samples `HitSweep.followArc` splits the step where the blade moves farthest at its clip-time midpoint, solving a sub-sample through Pose, until neither end of the blade moves more than `combat.sweepMaxTipStep` (equal to `hitSphereRadius`, 1.2) or `combat.sweepMaxSubdivisions` (12 per fighter per server frame, shared by every window that frame sweeps; it was 4 until the joint read below) is spent. Each server frame reads a fighter's joints once (`Pose.chain`) and every solve of that frame, in every window it sweeps, goes through that list; nothing is kept to the next frame, because respawns, equips, sheathing and accessories change the joints. Sub-samples are overlap-tested and cast like frame samples and share the window's hit set and target cap. Where the cap leaves a step longer than the limit, `castBladePath` also casts the chord of the tip or the guard, whichever moved that far. A sub-sample's root is interpolated between the two frames' roots, and in a hitch frame each frame sample's root is spread from the previous frame's last sample to the root the body had at the frame's last sample time (the live root, or, when the window closed earlier in the frame, where that straight walk had reached at the close); the root is never frozen during a window. `tools/validators/combat.luau` keeps the step at or under two radii. Extends D-010 and D-042. | An ExpoOut snap carries about 90% of its cut in one 60 Hz step (chords up to 11.7 studs on the Vertical), so straight chords cut the corner of the arc: a Lune study put 0.3-stud targets on the true blade at 44,568 placements (every Phase 0 window, 4 frame phases); the old chords missed 739 of 24,760 comparable placements, cap 4 with midpoint casts 57, cap 4 with end chords 0. A one-radius step means a blade that passes through a target touches it at a sample with half a radius to spare while the cap lasts. The snap frames need 5 to 22 sub-samples over 100 frame phases (Vertical 22, Rising Arc 21, Sharp Nail 18, Basic 3 17, Horizontal 12, Basic 2 6, Basic 1 5), so cap 12 binds only on the first four; at cap 4 the true blade strayed up to 0.94 studs from every sample and cast (0.26 of the radius left) and the end chords reached up to 0.39 studs past it, while at cap 12 the Vertical misses only grazes 0.09 to 0.14 deep and registers no hit outside the true blade's radius. No real-fight placement misses (hit matrix, 12,000 hitch swings, 540 rewound basics).
